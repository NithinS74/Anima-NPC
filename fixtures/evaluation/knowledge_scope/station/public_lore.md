# Meridian Station public lore

## The station

Meridian Station is a ring-built trading post in orbit around the gas giant Sarn. It was founded ninety years ago as a fuel depot and has grown to six habitat rings and fourteen docks. Visitors may use the outer docks and the market ring after clearing customs. The command ring is closed to all but staff and those summoned by the commander's office. Each quarter the station clerks publish the year's notices on the public boards, and residents may file petitions through the same terminals.

## Trade lanes

Three charted lanes connect Meridian to the outer colonies. The northern lane is the shortest and passes through the old mine fields. The southern lane is longer and was widely used during the last peace. A survey lane to the east is rarely used because of debris. Pilots pay a transit fee at each beacon. Safe passages around Sarn were few even before the blockade, and several navigation beacons have since been destroyed by raiders from both sides.

## The Kessel blockade

The Kessel Combine is a mining cartel from the outer belt, led by a board of shareholders. The present blockade began eight months ago when Combine ships seized a Meridian ore hauler in a dispute over mining rights. The station has lost two outposts and retaken one. Official bulletins announce each success on the public boards, and the families of those lost receive a small ration credit. Neither side has offered terms in public, and traders say Combine patrols have moved closer to Sarn this year.

## Fees and credit

The station collects a dock fee each quarter and a levy on cargo at customs. In emergencies the commander may also impose special fees, as earlier commanders did during the Reactor Strike. Station scrip is issued by the customs bank in the market ring, and the scrip mark is the common unit of account. Debts to the station are recorded by ring clerks. Haulers often pay in cargo rather than scrip, and clerks are widely distrusted for their inspections.

## The militia

Each ring must supply one in every twenty able residents to the station militia during an emergency. Militia members serve for a single rotation and are expected to return to their trades, although many do not. They train with sidearms and vacuum suits under security sergeants. Security posts are kept at Dock Nine, the market ring and the reactor spine, and their officers report through the command office. Fleets at Meridian have traditionally sortied after the solar season, once sensor interference has faded.

## Commander Hale

Commander Hale took office at thirty-four and has held the post for nineteen years. Hale is known for a love of old star charts and for settling the Reactor Strike by negotiation rather than force. Hale's partner died eleven years ago, and their only child serves as harbour master at the outer colonies. In earlier years the commander toured every ring each season. Station newsletters praise Hale's patience, while critics call the commander slow to act. Portraits of past commanders line the market ring.

## Customs and festivals

Residents of Meridian mark Founders' Day, when the first fuel depot was commissioned, and it is the largest festival of the year. Chaplains bless new ships before their first voyage and keep the birth and death registers for each ring. During the blockade, chapels have also collected rations for widows. Travelling speakers sometimes argue against the blockade policy, and the command office tolerates them so long as they do not name the commander.

## Private fleets and foreign trade

Private fleets have fought in the belt wars for generations. They sell their service by the contract and are known by their colours, such as the Red Comet and the Iron Bell line. Station law forbids them to dock under arms. Foreign haulers bring water, alloys and medical stock along the lanes, and water in particular has grown costly since the blockade closed the ice routes. Guilds on the market ring have complained to the command office about the price of alloys.
